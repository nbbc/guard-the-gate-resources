!

Filtering and accountability are not the same thing.

Filtering tries to block harmful content before it reaches the screen. Accountability brings another person into the process through reports, alerts, and honest conversation. Children need protection, not just reports.

Build layers of protection

The recommended protection stack

No single product covers every device, network, app, and situation. Each tool below serves a different purpose.

01

Home network filter · Start free

CleanBrowsing Family Filter

DNS layer

CleanBrowsing’s free Family Filter is a practical first network layer. It is designed to block adult content, enforce SafeSearch, and block common proxy or VPN domains across devices using the home connection.

  • Can cover many devices at once when configured on the router
  • Useful for smart TVs, game systems, and devices without filtering apps
  • Does not protect a phone using cellular data or another Wi-Fi network

02

Router / firewall control · Featured tool

Firewalla

Whole-home layer

Firewalla is the central whole-home control layer in this guide. It adds network visibility, user and device organization, schedules, blocking rules, Safe Search, and content controls.

  • Group devices by person and apply rules in one place
  • Pause internet access or schedule access by time
  • Protects the home network, but still needs device-level controls away from home
Setup note: If you also use CleanBrowsing, follow the vendors’ current DNS guidance so two network-filtering configurations do not compete.

03

Device filtering · Phones, tablets & laptops

Canopy

Device layer

Canopy is the featured device-level filtering option. It is designed to detect and filter explicit visual content in real time, and it offers app or website controls, downtime, and removal protection.

  • Protection can follow supported devices beyond the home network
  • Useful where a simple blocked-site list is not enough
  • Install and test it on every device that needs coverage

04

Personal accountability · Human support

Covenant Eyes

Accountability layer

Covenant Eyes combines filtering with Screen Accountability so a trusted ally can be part of the process. It is best understood as a relationship tool, not as the primary child-protection layer.

  • Useful for a man, spouse, or older teen seeking personal accountability
  • Works best with regular, gracious, honest conversation
  • Features and visibility vary by device and operating system
Use transparently: Accountability tools can report sensitive activity. Everyone involved should understand what is collected, who can see it, and why it is being used.

05

Family rules · Regular conversation

Shepherd the heart

Essential layer

Software can reduce exposure and make wise boundaries easier to enforce. It cannot replace a parent who listens, teaches, models self-control, and makes home the safest place to tell the truth.

“If you ever see something inappropriate, frightening, or uncomfortable, come tell me right away. You will not be in trouble for telling me. I want to help and protect you.”

Do not skip the free controls

Use what your devices already include

Built-in controls help with screen time, app installs, age ratings, communication, purchases, and account settings. Turn them on even when you use a paid product.

A practical plan

Do these seven things this week

  1. 1

    Inventory every internet-capable device. Include old phones, smart TVs, game systems, watches, VR headsets, and school devices.

  2. 2

    Put a filter on the home network. Begin with CleanBrowsing or configure Firewalla’s current content controls.

  3. 3

    Turn on built-in controls. Lock settings with a parent-only passcode and use child accounts.

  4. 4

    Protect mobile devices. Add device-level filtering where protection must work away from home.

  5. 5

    Remove screens from bedrooms. Charge devices publicly at night and set a device curfew.

  6. 6

    Test your setup. Check the home Wi-Fi, cellular data, guest networks, private browsing, apps, and every browser.

  7. 7

    Write the rules and start the conversation. Tell children what to do when harmful content appears.

Before choosing a product

Check the fit, not just the feature list.

  • Confirm support for every device and operating system in your home.
  • Review what data the tool collects, how long it keeps it, and who can see it.
  • Understand subscriptions, hardware requirements, and return policies on the vendor’s current site.
  • Use separate parent and child accounts. Keep admin passwords and recovery methods secure.
  • Re-test after router, phone, browser, and operating-system updates.
